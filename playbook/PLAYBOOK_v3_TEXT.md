**H2:** One rule change moved your fee

**BODY:**

## PAGE 04: KILLER 01

**EYEBROW:** DEAL-KILLER 01
**H1:** The valuation gap

**THE FILE:** The price is agreed and the deposit is paid. The bank's valuer comes in below the price, and the bank lends on the lower number.

**THE NUMBER:** A gap of around 10% is common. On an AED 4m purchase, that is AED 400,000 in cash nobody planned for.¹

**WHY THE BANK SAYS NO:** The bank chooses the valuer and treats the valuation as a hard limit. The buyer only sees it after the MoU is already running.

**WHAT IT COSTS YOU:** If the price drops to the valuation, your commission drops with it. In a rising market the seller may simply relist, and the deal is gone.

**WHERE NEMAX FITS:** If the buyer or a family member already owns a property in the UAE, NEMAX can raise capital against it to cover the gap. The deal closes at the agreed price, and you are paid on the agreed price.

**ASK EARLY:** Which bank is on this file, and has anyone seen a recent valuation in this building?

## PAGE 05: KILLER 02

**EYEBROW:** DEAL-KILLER 02
**H1:** The non-resident limit

**THE FILE:** A strong buyer with money abroad and no UAE residency. The bank's offer asks for a much bigger down payment than planned, and the deposit is already with the agency.

**THE NUMBER:** Banks usually lend non-residents 60 to 65% of the valuation, compared with up to 80% for residents. So the buyer needs 35 to 40% in cash.¹

**WHY THE BANK SAYS NO:** The limit depends on the buyer's residency, not on the property. The same home bought by a resident gets more.

**WHAT IT COSTS YOU:** Your strongest buyers on paper get stuck here, and they get stuck late, after the MoU is signed and the seller is counting on the date.

**WHERE NEMAX FITS:** NEMAX looks at the property, not the passport. If there is a UAE asset to secure against, the missing part can be covered. One more thing to know: this limit is bank policy, not a Central Bank rule, so a second bank is worth a call too.

**ASK EARLY:** Is the buyer a resident, and how much of their cash is already in the UAE?

## PAGE 06: KILLER 03

**EYEBROW:** DEAL-KILLER 03
**H1:** The off-plan gap

**THE FILE:** An off-plan unit. The developer's plan asks for 20 to 30% across booking and the first instalments. The buyer assumed a mortgage would cover it.

**THE NUMBER:** Banks do not lend until construction is about 50 to 60% complete. The first half of the payment plan is on the buyer.¹

**WHY THE BANK SAYS NO:** An unfinished unit is not security the bank accepts. Since mid-2026 a few developer and bank programmes start earlier, but only for their own projects and only after half the plan is paid. Most mid-market developers have no such programme.²

**WHAT IT COSTS YOU:** The buyer pays the middle of the plan alone or walks away. Either way the file goes quiet for a year, and the booking is all you were paid on.

**WHERE NEMAX FITS:** If the buyer owns another UAE property, NEMAX can raise short-term capital against it to cover the construction instalments until a bank can step in.

**ASK EARLY:** Which developer, and does this exact project have a named bank partner?

## PAGE 07: KILLER 04

**EYEBROW:** DEAL-KILLER 04
**H1:** The handover shortfall

**THE FILE:** Handover is called and the final payment is due. The buyer's money is tied up in another property that has not sold yet. No final payment, no NOC, no transfer.

**THE NUMBER:** If the buyer defaults, the developer can keep up to 25% of the price when the project is under 60% complete, and up to 40% above that.¹

**WHY THE BANK SAYS NO:** The law sets what the developer keeps, whatever the reason the buyer is short. The developer's process runs on its own timeline: grace period, late fees, then a notice through the DLD.

**WHAT IT COSTS YOU:** No transfer means no commission. And a client who has just lost a quarter of what they paid will not come back to you for the next purchase.

**WHERE NEMAX FITS:** NEMAX can cover the final payment with capital secured on property. The unit is handed over, and the other property sells in its own time instead of at a discount.

**ASK EARLY:** When will handover be called, and where exactly is the final payment coming from?

## PAGE 08: KILLER 05

**EYEBROW:** DEAL-KILLER 05
**H1:** The income cap

**THE FILE:** The buyer owns property outright, runs a business and pays themselves a modest salary. On paper they are your safest client. The bank says no.

**THE NUMBER:** The Central Bank caps monthly debt payments at 50% of monthly income, and unused credit-card limits count against it.¹

**WHY THE BANK SAYS NO:** The cap is a rule about the person's income. A strong property cannot change it, because the property is not what is being measured.

**WHAT IT COSTS YOU:** The buyers with real assets are exactly the ones the bank turns down. You lose the deal and the referrals that would have come after it.

**WHERE NEMAX FITS:** NEMAX starts from the property: what it is worth and what it can support. A file that is dead at the bank can still work here, because the question is different.

**ASK EARLY:** What income does the buyer declare, and how many unused credit cards are in their name?

## PAGE 09: KILLER 06

**EYEBROW:** DEAL-KILLER 06
**H1:** The MoU clock

**THE FILE:** The MoU is signed and the clock is running. The bank is still asking for documents, and the seller has stopped replying about an extension.

**THE NUMBER:** A Form F MoU usually runs about 30 days. Bank approval for a buyer with foreign or self-employed income usually takes four to six weeks. The two do not fit.¹

**WHY THE BANK SAYS NO:** It is not one slow bank. Checking foreign income takes time everywhere, and the Form F clock was never built around it.

**WHAT IT COSTS YOU:** When the MoU expires, the seller is free. Sometimes you get an extension. Often a cash buyer has been waiting for exactly this moment.

**WHERE NEMAX FITS:** NEMAX can put capital in place for transfer day, so the buyer completes like a cash buyer and moves to a bank afterwards. NEMAX targets around 30 days from application to capital in place. That is the low end of the market, so it helps most when the file comes in early.

**ASK EARLY:** What date is on the Form F, and is the buyer's income foreign or self-employed?

## PAGE 13: APPENDIX: THREE LATE PROBLEMS

**EYEBROW:** APPENDIX
**H1:** Three problems that show up late.

**BODY:** These appear after the money is sorted, which is why they hurt. All three can be spotted before the MoU is signed.

**The developer's NOC.** Unpaid service charges or an open dispute, and the eNOC is not issued. No eNOC, no DLD transfer, and the appointment is cancelled while the MoU keeps running. The certificate costs AED 500 to 5,000 plus VAT and takes two to five working days.¹
*Ask for the service-charge statement on paper before you list.*

**The seller's mortgage.** When the seller still has a mortgage, the transfer can take up to around 60 days. The bank's liability letter can take 14 days to arrive and is valid only for a short time. If it expires, it has to be issued again, and the clock restarts.²
*Ask which bank holds the mortgage before the MoU is signed.*

**The cooling-off clawback.** The seven days or so that buyers talk about on off-plan come from the developer's contract, not from RERA. If the buyer cancels inside that window after the SPA is signed, the developer takes the commission back.³
*Read the cancellation clause before you count the fee as earned.*

**FOOTNOTES:** 1. F&P Property; EGSH; DLD REST / Oqood. 2. Allsopp & Allsopp; 800homes. 3. Betterhomes. There is no legal cooling-off period for off-plan.

## PAGE 14: APPENDIX: BEFORE YOU LIST

**EYEBROW:** APPENDIX
**H1:** Five questions that save most of this.

**BODY:** Every problem in this playbook is cheaper to find before the listing than after the MoU.

**01 Get the service-charge position on paper.** Not what the owner remembers. Unpaid charges stop the eNOC, and that is the most common reason a transfer date moves.

**02 Ask if there is a mortgage, and with which bank.** This decides on day one whether the transfer takes two weeks or two months.

**03 Agree in writing who pays the 4%.** The law splits it. In practice the buyer pays. If nobody says it, it turns into an argument on transfer day.

**04 Give the full cost in dirhams at the first meeting.** Roughly 6.5 to 8% of the price on a cash purchase, more with a mortgage, across six items. A buyer who hears it early plans for it. A buyer who hears it late asks you to cut your fee.

**05 Ask when the buyer's cash will actually arrive.** Not whether they have it. Money waiting on another sale, or sitting abroad for three weeks, is no money on the day.
